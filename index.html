Cleaning, Laundry & Household Chores

A 40-minute private lesson for Vinicius · Level B2 · Vocabulary, frequency adverbs and quantifiers, guided writing and speaking. Yellow boxes are for the teacher; open them as needed.

Lesson overview

Objectives

  • Use ten household words and several B2 expressions accurately.
  • Place adverbs of frequency correctly and choose the right quantifier.
  • Write and then explain a detailed, personal household routine.

Success criteria

  • Writes 120–160 words with ≥5 frequency expressions, ≥4 quantifiers, ≥6 target words and ≥2 complex sentences.
  • Speaks without reading the text and justifies decisions.
  • Corrects at least one of his own mistakes.

Teacher notes: general approach

Student-centred and inductive: elicit first, explain last. Never assume Vinicius owns a dishwasher, shares a flat or has a washing machine at home (in Frankfurt many buildings have a shared Waschkeller). If an item doesn't apply, ask him to talk about what he does instead. Note recurring errors on paper during Stages 1, 2 and 5; give feedback only in Stage 6.

Stage 2 · Name That Chore!8 min

First guess the word from the clue. Then open the card to check.

🔌
vacuum
Check

verb: to clean with a machine that sucks up dust. noun: a vacuum cleaner.

“I vacuum the living room every Saturday.”

vacuum the carpet · run the vacuum

Clue: You use this to remove dust and dirt from carpets.

🪣
mop
Check

A tool with a long handle for cleaning floors with water. Also a verb.

“I mop the kitchen floor after cooking.”

mop the floor · mop and bucket

Clue: You use this to clean the floor with water.

🧹
broom
Check

A brush with a long handle for sweeping dry dirt. The verb is sweep.

“Grab the broom and sweep the hallway.”

broom and dustpan · sweep the floor

Clue: You use this to sweep dry dirt into a pile.

🧺
laundry
Check

Dirty clothes to wash, or the activity of washing them. Uncountable.

“I do the laundry on Sundays.”

do the laundry · sort the laundry · a load of laundry

Clue: This is what you wash, and the chore of washing it.

🧴
detergent
Check

Liquid, powder or pods that clean clothes (or dishes). Uncountable.

“We're almost out of detergent.”

laundry detergent · a scoop of detergent

Clue: You use this product to wash your clothes.

🌸
fabric softener
Check

A liquid that makes clothes soft and fresh-smelling after washing.

“I don't often use fabric softener.”

add fabric softener · scented fabric softener

Clue: It makes washed clothes soft and nice-smelling.

🍽️
dishwasher
Check

A machine that washes plates and glasses.

“There are a lot of dishes in the dishwasher.”

load / unload / run the dishwasher

Clue: A machine that washes your plates.

👕
hang clothes
Check

To put wet clothes on a line or rack so they dry. Past: hung.

“I hang my clothes out to dry on the balcony.”

hang out the washing · drying rack

Clue: What you do with wet clothes instead of using a dryer.

👔
fold clothes
Check

To make clean clothes flat and neat before putting them away.

“I have too many clothes to fold.”

fold and put away · a pile of clothes

Clue: You do this to clean T-shirts before they go in the drawer.

🗑️
take out the trash
Check

To carry rubbish outside to the bins. In Frankfurt you also sort and recycle it.

“I take out the trash every other day.”

sort the recycling · empty the bin

Clue: You carry the rubbish outside.

Laundry: three different actions

do the laundry = the whole process (wash, dry, fold). hang the clothes out to dry = put wet clothes on a line or rack. fold the clothes = make dry clothes neat.

Teacher notes

Minutes: 3 guess + check, 3 sentences, 2 describe-it. Read the clues aloud (auditory) and let him mime the action (kinesthetic). Stage 3 · Spot the Pattern8 min

“I usually do the laundry on Saturdays. I rarely use fabric softener, and I hardly ever leave dirty dishes in the sink. I have a few cleaning products in the bathroom, but there isn't much detergent left. I often vacuum the living room because we have a lot of dust.”
  1. Find the frequency adverbs.
  2. Rank them from most to least frequent.
  3. Find the quantifiers.
  4. Which nouns are countable? Which are uncountable?
  5. Where do the adverbs go in the sentence?

Reference

Frequency scale (approximate)

always › usually › often › sometimes › occasionally › rarely › hardly ever › never

Position: before the main verb (I usually do…), after be (It is usually tidy), after the auxiliary (I don't often use…).

Adverb vs. expression: I rarely vacuum. / I vacuum twice a week.

Quantifiers
  • Countable: many, a few, too many, enough, some/any (chores, clothes, products)
  • Uncountable: much, a little, too much, enough, some/any (detergent, laundry, time)
  • Both: a lot of. Use some in positive sentences, any in negatives and questions.

Common mistakes: I vacuum rarely · too much clothes · many detergent

Correction challenge

Correct each sentence and explain why.

  1. I vacuum rarely.
  2. My flat usually is tidy.
  3. I have too much clothes to fold.
  4. There aren't many detergent left.
  5. I don't have some cleaning products.
Teacher notes

2 min text and tasks 1–5, 2 min reference (let him state the rules first), 3 min corrections. Treat clothes as plural countable (no singular “a clothe”), laundry and detergent as uncountable. Answers are in the answer-key section; don't show them yet.

Stage 4 · My Household Routine10 min

Think first (2 min), then write (8 min). Choose at least 12 questions and answer about your real life. If something doesn't apply, say what you do instead.

Cleaning habits
  • How often do you sweep your home?
  • How often do you vacuum or mop?
  • How often do you wipe down the countertops?
  • How often do you clean the bathroom?
Laundry
  • How often do you do the laundry?
  • Do you sort your clothes before washing?
  • How much detergent do you use? Fabric softener?
  • Do you hang your clothes out to dry?
  • Do you fold clothes immediately or leave a pile?
Kitchen & daily chores
  • How often do you run the dishwasher or wash up?
  • How often do you take out the trash?
  • Do you have enough storage for cleaning supplies?
  • Which chore takes the most time?
Preferences
  • Which chores do you do during the week, and which do you save for the weekend?
  • Which chore do you hardly ever do?
  • What would you like to change?
Optional extension questions
  • How many cleaning products do you have at home?
  • Which task do you find the most annoying, and why?

Sentence starters

I usually…I hardly ever…Once or twice a week, I…I spend a lot of time…I don't have enough…There are too many…I need a little more…One thing I'd like to change is…

Worksheet: My Household Routine

Write 120–160 words in total. Include ≥5 frequency expressions, ≥4 quantifiers, ≥6 target words and ≥2 sentences with a reason (because, so, although…).

Teacher notes

Stay quiet while he writes; offer vocabulary only if asked and circle errors silently. If time runs short, he completes sections 1–4 and finishes the rest as homework. The live counter under the worksheet is automatic and approximate (it spots exact words), so use it as a guide, not a grade. Pay attention to adverb position and much/many.

Stage 5 · Explain Your Routine7 min

Explain your routine naturally. Use your notes only for reference; don't read them aloud.

Follow-up questions

  • Why do you usually clean on that particular day?
  • Which chore takes up the most time?
  • What happens if you don't do the laundry for a week?
  • Do you think you spend too much time cleaning?
  • How would your routine change with two roommates?
  • What products would you recommend to someone in their first apartment?
  • How could you make your routine more efficient?

Surprise scenario

You've had an extremely busy week. Your laundry is piling up, the kitchen is messy, and you've run out of detergent. You only have 30 minutes. What do you do first, and why?

Teacher notes

3 min free explanation, 2 min follow-ups, 2 min scenario. Push for justification: “Why that first?” Useful language: first of all, otherwise, as a result, that way. Observe: vocabulary range, adverb placement, quantifiers, elaboration, fluency (see checklist). Sample answer: buy detergent on the way home, start one load, run the dishwasher while it washes, then wipe down surfaces.

Answer keys

Open teacher answer keys

Stage 3 text

  1. Adverbs: usually, rarely, hardly ever, often.
  2. Most to least frequent: usually › often › rarely › hardly ever.
  3. Quantifiers: a few (cleaning products), much (detergent), a lot of (dust).
  4. Countable: dishes, cleaning products. Uncountable: detergent, dust, laundry.
  5. The adverbs sit before the main verb (usually do, rarely use, often vacuum); hardly ever goes before leave.

Correction challenge

  1. I rarely vacuum. Frequency adverbs go before the main verb, not after it.
  2. My flat is usually tidy. Adverbs go after be.
  3. too many clothes. Clothes is countable and plural.
  4. There isn't much detergent left. Detergent is uncountable.
  5. I don't have any cleaning products. Use any in negatives (or: I have some cleaning products).
